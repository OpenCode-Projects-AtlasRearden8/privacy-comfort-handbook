# The printable privacy checklist

*This page is designed to print on one or two sheets. Press the print button above, or Ctrl+P (Cmd+P on a Mac). Tick the boxes on paper, or tick them here on screen before printing.*

Every item has a chapter reference in brackets, in case you want the why before the how. Nothing on this list needs to be done today, this week, or ever all at once. Start at the top and drift downward at your own pace.

## The 15-minute version: today

- [ ] Pick your two most important accounts — email and bank — and turn on 2FA with an authenticator app or passkey. [Chapter 03]
- [ ] Read the never-paste list once, out loud, so it sticks ("passwords, codes, ID numbers, card numbers"). [Chapter 02]
- [ ] Update your browser and phone if either has been nagging you about it. [Chapter 05]

## The one-hour version: this weekend

- [ ] Install or switch on a password manager; move your top ten logins in as you naturally visit each site. [Chapter 03]
- [ ] Turn on 2FA for anything else holding money or people: payment apps, cloud backup, social media. [Chapter 03]
- [ ] Save your 2FA recovery codes somewhere calm — printed or in the password manager's secure notes. [Chapter 03]
- [ ] Run your phone's permission audit: location, camera, microphone, contacts — "while using the app," "ask every time," or "never." [Chapter 05]
- [ ] In your browser: block third-party cookies, then prune extensions down to the two or three you actually chose. [Chapter 05]
- [ ] Check your email address at haveibeenpwned.com and run the five-step drill on anything it lists. [Chapter 07]

## The household pass: family and kids

- [ ] Put recovery codes and an account list in one family emergency folder. [Chapter 06]
- [ ] Make the household vow: any request for money or codes goes through one named person first. [Chapter 06]
- [ ] Set up child accounts with parental approval for kids' new apps and purchases. [Chapter 06]
- [ ] On shared devices: separate profiles, and a sign-out habit after email or banking. [Chapter 06]
- [ ] Have the one-minute talk: "a message sent can be forwarded" (kids and teens), or "call me before paying anyone new" (grandparents). [Chapter 06]

## The twice-a-year ritual

- [ ] Re-check your main email addresses at haveibeenpwned.com. [Chapter 07]
- [ ] Open your password manager's security page and fix anything it flags. [Chapter 03]
- [ ] Run the phone permission audit once more; revoke anything that crept back. [Chapter 05]
- [ ] Check browser and phone updates, and let them finish installing. [Chapter 05]

## The someday-even list (drift here occasionally)

- [ ] Consider a small reputable VPN for travel and hotel Wi-Fi only. [Chapter 05]
- [ ] Look up how to place a credit freeze in your country, and keep the instructions handy even if you never use them. [Chapter 02]
- [ ] Retire your oldest online accounts you no longer use — closed accounts cannot leak. [Chapter 07]
- [ ] Skim the [glossary](09-glossary.md) once; knowing the words makes every future privacy story less alarming. [Extras]

## The emergency card: if something goes wrong

- [ ] Typed a password into the wrong place? Change it now, sign out all sessions, check for anything newly added to the account. [Chapter 04]
- [ ] Gave out a 2FA code? Same drill, immediately, plus check for unknown devices and forwarding rules. [Chapter 04]
- [ ] Sent a card number or ID detail somewhere it should not go? Call the card issuer or set a fraud alert — calm, official, free. [Chapter 02]
- [ ] A payment actually happened? Call your bank with the number on your card and the words "I was scammed"; then report at your country's official site only when you feel like it. [Chapter 04]

## The one-line version, for the fridge

Long unique passwords, 2FA on what matters, codes stay yours alone, urgency is a red flag, check twice a year, and otherwise — carry on with your life.

> **Remember:** the point of this list was never to complete it. The point is that you now know where the doorways are, and you can close the ones you care about in whatever order suits the season of your life you are in.
